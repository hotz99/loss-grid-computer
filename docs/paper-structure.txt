Abstract: although this section is the first one of your thesis, it must be the last section you write. This section summarizes the main aspects of your thesis and it should include: i) the description of the problem you tacked, ii) a brief description of the method(s) used, iii) the major findings or results, and iv) their interpretation and conclusions.

Introduction: the introduction must include the explanation of the problem you investigated, where the research in this report is placed in the existing literature and in terms of the state of the art, requiring a literature study. Furthermore, a problem statement is formulated and research questions are posed. Here you also sketch the structure of the report.

Methods: this section must contain a wide description of your approach. If needed this can be spread over multiple sections.

Experiments: the description of what you want to find out and how and why you have designed your experiments are placed here.

Results: in this section, you report the results of your experiments, including proper statistical analysis. Here the findings should be stated without bias or interpretation.

Discussion: in this section, your interpretation of the results of your experiments are required. It is also important to place them in terms of what is known from the literature.

Conclusions: you draw your conclusions from your research and answer your research questions in this section. Also, reflect on the societal implications.

References: to develop your work, you generally make use of or get inspired by ideas, methodologies or software from others. You should always cite these sources in your report. This section is intended for it.
